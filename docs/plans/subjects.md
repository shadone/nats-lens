Working plan for the Subjects page (`docs/features/subjects.md`, board `Subjects.dc.html` of the design canvas). Everything here was proposed by the planning session of 2026-10-22 and is not yet confirmed by the owner. When a task lands, its lasting content moves into `docs/features/subjects.md`, `docs/features/monitoring-snapshots.md` and `docs/design.md`; this file then only tracks status.

## Rules for every step

- Read-only towards NATS, as everywhere. The page never subscribes to an application subject and never asks a server live; it reads the store, so a past range works as a live one.
- A value a server did not report is unset, never 0. "No subscriber" and "subscriptions not collected" are different states and the page says which.
- The page shows the newest collection at or before the end of the page range (the epoch rule of `GetPlacement`), and the newest subscription collection at or before that epoch.
- No message rate per subject: NATS does not count one. Rates come from a stream's sequence growth, and from the JetStream API counters for the `$JS.API.>` row.

## What backs each part

| Part | Source | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Stream rows | `stream_config` and `stream_stats` of the epoch | One row per (account, stream) with its subjects, from the leader's configuration (else the first reporting server by name, the rule of the Capacity page). KV buckets (`KV_`) and object stores (`OBJ_`) by the prefix rule of `ListKeyValueStores`; their subjects are the ones the stream configuration lists (`$KV.<bucket>.>`, `$O.<bucket>.C.>`, `$O.<bucket>.M.>`). A mirror has no subjects of its own: its row has an empty pattern cell and reads "mirror of `<origin>`". |
| Stored msgs/s | leader `stream_stats.last_seq` | The increase of the leader's last sequence per second over the 15 minutes before the range end, in the newest run of one leader and one creation time, as `deliveredRates` reads a consumer's delivered sequence. Fewer than two rows: unset (an en dash). |
| Stream subscribers | `consumer_config`, `consumer_stats` of the epoch, the subscription collection | The stream's consumers: "6 durable consumers", "1 ephemeral", "2 durable, 1 ephemeral"; on a KV bucket the ephemeral consumers are "watchers". A push consumer whose `deliver_subject` has a core subscriber in the subscription collection names that connection: "1 ephemeral (`<connection name>`)", at most 3 names, then "+N". No consumer: "none". |

Subscriptions on `_INBOX.` subjects fold into one row per account, pattern `_INBOX.>`, note "reply inboxes". |
| Server subscriptions | the subscription collection | A subscription whose connection ID is not a client connection of the same server in the same epoch's CONNZ is the server's own (JetStream, system and account internal clients) when that server's CONNZ listed every connection; it is left out of the rows and counted in the footnote. When CONNZ was cut at `connz_limit`, such a subscription is counted as "connection not listed" in the footnote, not guessed. |
| JetStream API row | `account_stats.js_api_total`, `js_api_errors` | One row per account with JetStream use: pattern `$JS.API.>`, subscribers "server", note "JetStream API: 3.1 req/s, 0.2% errors" from the counters' increase over the 15 minutes before the range end. Every server reports the account's counters for the whole cluster, so one server's increase is taken (the highest, in its newest run), not the sum (corrected in T2's fix round). Independent of the subscription collection. |
| Note | the row's facts | First that applies: overlap ("overlaps `<other>`", warning), stream without a consumer (below, warning), "work queue, empty", "interest retention: kept only while someone listens", "mirror of `<origin>`", "sourced into `<stream>`", "receives every message in the account" (a subscription on `>`), "reply inboxes". |
| Filters | the rows | All patterns; Captured by a stream (rows with a stream); Stream without a consumer (a stream with no consumer that no other stream mirrors or sources, KV buckets and object stores excluded; an interest stream reads "messages are dropped", a work queue "messages wait for a consumer", a limits stream "messages are kept but nobody reads them"); Overlapping streams (a stream row whose subjects overlap another stream's in the same account, wildcards included); Wide subscriptions (`>`) (subscription rows whose subject ends in `>`, the folded inbox row excluded). Each pill shows its count over all rows, not the cut list. |

Server subscriptions are left out. A subject that nothing matches: "no stream stores it and no subscriber listens". |

Order of the rows: rows with a warning first; then by account (the system account last); within an account, stream rows before subscription rows; stream rows by stored rate, descending, then pattern; subscription rows by connections, descending, then pattern. At most 1,000 rows, with the total and each filter's count.

Footnote: the board's text, plus the subscription collection's time ("subscriptions as of 14:05") and, when it applies, servers that did not answer, servers whose list was cut at the limit ("nats-1 listed 10,000 of 48,211"), and the count of server subscriptions left out. No subscription collection within 24 hours before the epoch: "Subscriptions not collected" and subscription rows are absent; stream rows still show their consumers.

### One row per stream, not per pattern

The feature doc says one row per subject pattern. A stream with several subjects has one rate and one set of consumers, so a row per pattern would repeat them and the rate would read as each pattern's own. Proposed, not yet confirmed: one row per stream with every pattern in its first cell, one per line; the tester names the one pattern that matched.

## T1 Collect subscription lists

- **Request.** A new endpoint `SUBSZ` (`$SYS.REQ.SERVER.PING.SUBSZ`) with `server.SubszOptions{Subscriptions: true, Limit: <subsz_limit>}`. It is asked in an epoch only when the last subscription collection started at least `subscriptions_interval` ago (the collector keeps the time, as `accountAsked` does), alongside the detail endpoints in the same window, so the epoch takes no longer. It is marked missing for a server only in an epoch in which it was asked.
- **Config.** `subscriptions_interval` (duration, default 5 minutes, 0 turns the collection off) and `subsz_limit` (the most subscriptions stored per server per collection, default 10,000), with flags, as `connz_limit` is. Validation and the `run` startup log name them.
- **What SUBSZ lists.** nats-server 2.15 lists each account's local subscriptions (`Sublist.localSubs` without leaf hubs): clients, and the server's own system, JetStream and account clients; not route or gateway interest, so a subscription is listed once, by the server its client is connected to. The implementer confirms this with an in-process cluster test.
- **Storage.** Migration `0032_subscriptions.sql`:
  - table `subscriptions` (`epoch`, `server_id`, `account`, `subject`, `queue` (NULL for none), `cid`, `subs` (subscriptions with this subject, queue and connection), `msgs` (messages delivered to them, summed)), one row per (epoch, server, account, subject, queue, cid), with comments on every column;
  - table `subscription_lists` (`epoch`, `server_id`, `total` (the server's `num_subscriptions`), `listed` (rows of the reply before folding)), one row per server that answered SUBSZ in the epoch.
  - Both follow `connections` in retention and in the hourly archive (`epochTables`). A rebuild from hours archived before 0032 must still work.
- **No JWT, no payload.** SUBSZ carries neither; nothing else is kept.
- **Docs.** `docs/design.md` (what is collected and how often), `monitoring-snapshots.md`, `running-nats-lens.md` (the two options), the SQL access table list if it names tables.
- **Tests.** The interval (asked on the first epoch, not again before the interval, again after it; 0 never); the limit; folding of duplicate subscriptions of one connection; queue groups; missing marked only when asked; the migration applies on a store with data; archive and rebuild with and without the new tables; an in-process cluster test: a client's subscription is listed once in a 3-node cluster, with its account, queue and `cid` matching the CONNZ row.

### Where T1 departed from this plan

Each point was decided in the session that built T1: proposed, not yet confirmed.

- **`subscription_lists.total` is SUBSZ `total`, not `num_subscriptions`.** `num_subscriptions` counts every entry of the accounts' subject trees, route and gateway interest included: in the 3-node test cluster each server reported 217 to 242 against 73 to 98 local subscriptions. `total` is the number of local subscriptions before the limit, the figure "nats-1 listed 10,000 of 48,211" needs. `num_subscriptions` is not stored with the list; `server_stats.subscriptions` holds the VARZ value.
- **One second of slack.** A collection is due when the last one started at least `subscriptions_interval` less one second ago. Epoch starts jitter by milliseconds; without the slack a 5-minute interval at a 30-second epoch interval often became 5 minutes 30.
- **Asked when sent.** An epoch that sent SUBSZ counts as the last collection whether or not any server answered, as an account lookup does; a collection that no server answered is not retried before the interval.
- **What SUBSZ lists, confirmed.** `monitor.go` `Subsz` walks every account and calls `acc.sl.localSubs(&subs, false)`; `sublist.go` `addLocalSub` keeps a subscription whose client kind is `CLIENT`, `SYSTEM`, `JETSTREAM` or `ACCOUNT` (leaf hubs only when asked, which SUBSZ does not), so route, gateway and leaf node interest is never listed. Internal clients take their cid from the same counter as clients (`client.go` `initClient`), so they never share a CONNZ cid. In the 3-node test (`TestSubszClusterListsEachSubscriptionOnce`) a client's queue subscription was listed once, by its own server, with the account, queue group and cid of that server's CONNZ row; most of each server's 73 to 98 listed subscriptions were its own clients': in `SYS` the `$SYS.REQ.>` services (46), `$SYS.SERVER.>`, `$SYS.ACCOUNT.>`, `$NRG.>` raft subjects, `$JSC.>` and `$JS.API.>`; in each account, `$G` included, `$JS.API.>` and four `$SYS.REQ.>` service imports. A stream's subjects (`inventory.>`) were listed once, under the stream leader's JetStream client. T2's server-subscription rule covers all of these by cid.
- **Batched writes.** Subscriptions go to DuckDB as JSON batches of 5,000 rows (`from_json` and `unnest`, as the findings writer does) rather than one prepared insert per row; two lists of 10,000 are written in about 80 ms in memory (`TestSubscriptionsLargeList`).
- **Indexes and types.** `subscription_lists` has a unique index on (`epoch`, `server_id`); `subscriptions` none, as the other per-entity stats tables. `subs` is INTEGER, `msgs` BIGINT, `cid` BIGINT as in `connections`.
- **Account names.** SUBSZ gives each subscription's account name tag; the writer does not record it in `accounts`, which CONNZ, STATZ, JSZ and ACCOUNTZ fill.
- **Reply size.** A reply of 10,000 subscriptions measured about 1.5 MB in config mode and crossed a route to the collector without trouble (`TestSubszTenThousandOverARoute`); with 56-character account keys and name tags the field sizes make it about 2.3 MB. The server sends it as an internal reply, which `max_payload` does not limit. The collector's inbox has no pending limits, but its connection is bounded by the `max_pending` of the server it is connected to (64 MB by default): every server's SUBSZ and CONNZ replies converge on it in one window, and a single reply over `max_pending` closes the connection as a slow consumer (the reviewer's scaled test: a 1.5 MB reply against a 1 MB `max_pending` closed it and failed the whole epoch). Fix round 1 bounds `subsz_limit` at 100,000 and documents the interaction in `running-nats-lens.md`.
- **The start log.** It names `connz_limit` too, which it did not before.
- **The store fixture.** `fixtureSnapshot` in `internal/store` now carries a SUBSZ reply per server (nats-0 lists 5 of 7, with a folded queue pair, an inbox and an internal client's subscription; nats-1 lists 1), which T2's store tests can build on.
- **Fix round 1 (review of T1).** Migration 0032's comments were corrected in place and its golden checksum updated, as 0032 had been applied nowhere outside this branch: the global account is `$G` in `subscriptions` (as in `account_stats`) and empty in `connections`, so a subscription joins its connection on (`epoch`, `server_id`, `cid`) only; a cid also lacks a connection row when CONNZ did not answer that server in that epoch. `subsz_limit` is at most 100,000 (above). A clock stepped back makes a subscription collection due at once instead of waiting until the clock passes the last start; account lookups got the same one-condition fix. `docs/design.md` names the server's cost of a SUBSZ call and the stored size.
- **Docs beyond the list.** The README (configuration table, the keys shared by systems, the collector line, the table list and a paragraph on the two tables), `natslens.example.yaml`, the coverage line of monitoring snapshots in `docs/features/README.md`, and a `natslens sql` scenario in `monitoring-snapshots.md`.

## T2 API, store and demo

`proto/natslens/v1/subjects.proto`:

- RPC `GetSubjects(GetSubjectsRequest) returns (GetSubjectsResponse)`. Request: `system`, `range`, `account` (the scope, empty for all). Response: `epoch`, `epoch_at`, `optional subscriptions_at` (unset: not collected within 24 hours), `repeated SubjectRow rows`, `int32 rows_total`, the five filter counts, `repeated SubszServer` (server name, answered, `total`, `listed`) for the footnote, `int64 server_subscriptions`, `int64 unlisted_connection_subscriptions`.
- `SubjectRow`: `account`, `account_name`, `kind` (enum: stream, KV bucket, object store, subscription, inbox, JetStream API), `repeated string patterns`, `stream` (empty for none), `mirror_of`, `repeated string sourced_into`, `retention`, `int64 messages`, `int32 durable_consumers`, `int32 ephemeral_consumers`, `int32 connections`, `queue` (when every subscriber shares one), `repeated string client_names` (at most 3) and `int32 more_names`, `optional double stored_per_second`, `optional double api_per_second`, `optional double api_error_share`, `repeated string overlaps`, `bool no_consumer`, `bool wide`. The UI words the subscribers and the note from these fields.
- RPC `MatchSubject(MatchSubjectRequest) returns (MatchSubjectResponse)`. Request: `system`, `range`, `account`, `subject`. A subject with a wildcard, an empty token or over 255 bytes is `InvalidArgument`. Response: `repeated SubjectMatch matches`, each with `account`, `account_name`, `stream`, `pattern`, `repeated string consumers`, `repeated string mirrors`, `repeated string sourced_by`; and `repeated CoreMatch subscribers` (`account`, `subject`, `queue`, `int32 connections`, client names as above); `optional subscriptions_at`.
- Rules in `internal/api/subjects.go` (subject matching and overlap, the inbox fold, the server-subscription rule, the notes' facts, the order, the counts, the cut), shared by the store (`storeprov/subjects_page.go`, SQL aggregates for the subscription rows, so a list of 100,000 subscriptions is never read into Go row by row) and the demo, as `FinishCapacity` is. Subject matching may use nats-server's exported helpers (`server.SubjectsCollide`, `server.SubjectMatchesFilter`) if they fit.
- Handler: the common range validation and `QueryTimeout` through `call()`. No epoch in the range: the empty response with `epoch` 0.
- **Demo.** Each demo system gets subjects that tell the page's story with the shop's names: in `example-global` a stream without a consumer (a limits stream named for a test), an interest stream with one ephemeral push consumer whose connection is named, a work queue with no messages, a KV bucket with two watchers, a mirror, a queue group of three `order-service` connections, `_INBOX` subscriptions from several connections, natslens's own connection subscribed to wide `$SYS` subjects in the system account, and a subscription on `>` by a monitoring tool. One server's list is cut at the limit in `example-global`. `example-staging` has no subscription collection.
- Conformance rows in `apitest`: a stream row with its consumers and rate; a subscription row with a queue group; the inbox fold; a push consumer's deliver subject is not a row of its own and names its client on the stream row; a server subscription is left out when CONNZ is complete and counted as unlisted when it was cut; the filters' counts; overlap; the tester (stream, consumer filter, mirror, queue group once, nothing matches, a wildcard refused); no subscription collection; the store fixture and the demo pass alike.
- Docs: `docs/design.md` (the RPCs, the rules), `demo-mode.md`.

### Where T2 departed from this plan

Each point was decided in the session that built T2 and is proposed, not yet confirmed.

- **A filter in the request.** `GetSubjectsRequest` has `filter` (`SubjectFilter`: stored, no consumer, overlapping, wide), as `GetPlacement` has: the server filters before the cut of 1,000, so a pill shows every row it counts. The counts are `all_count`, `stored_count`, `no_consumer_count`, `overlapping_count` and `wide_count`; the footnote's servers are `subsz_servers`.
- **Fields the plan did not list.** `SubjectRow.mirrored_by` (streams that mirror the row's stream, beside `sourced_into`), `SubjectRow.system_account` (the order puts it last and the UI can say so), `SubjectMatch.ephemeral_consumers` (the tester names durables and counts ephemeral ones, whose names are random), and `epoch` and `epoch_at` on `MatchSubjectResponse`. `messages` is `optional`, unset when not reported.
- **A mirror is never "without a consumer".** A mirror kept as a copy is read by nobody on purpose; flagging every backup mirror would bury the real cases.
- **SUBSZ servers and totals.** `subsz_servers` lists the servers with a SUBSZ outcome in the collection and, unanswered, those the epoch missed. `total` is SUBSZ's own count of local subscriptions before the limit, not `num_subscriptions`, which counts route interest too.
- **The CONNZ rule.** A subscription without a connection is the server's own only when that server's CONNZ answered in the collection's epoch and was not cut; a CONNZ that did not answer counts as unlisted, as a cut one does. The join is on server and client ID only. The tester leaves unlisted connections out too, not only the servers' own.
- **JetStream use.** An account has a `$JS.API.>` row when it holds a stream or its `account_stats` report API requests or streams in the epoch.
- **Deliver subjects** are those of the page's epoch's push consumers, matched exactly.
- **Conformance.** The demo never cuts CONNZ, so "counted as unlisted when it was cut" is a store test (`TestSubjectsFromStore`, `TestSubjectsCollectionReach`), not a table row. NATS refuses overlapping stream subjects in one account, so the demo has none; the table checks that the overlap count is the overlapping rows' and that overlap is symmetric in every system, and the store fixture holds two overlapping streams.
- **The demo.** The KV story uses `KV_feature-flags`' existing 22 watchers, named by their connections, instead of a new bucket with two. The monitoring tool on `>` is `ops-monitor` in `SYS`, beside `natslens-example-global`; they are `example-global`'s new connections in `SYS`, so the Accounts page counts 2 connections there, the servers' and systems' connection counts include them, and the demo's `ListConnections` now hides tooling connections unless `include_tooling` is set, as the store does. Three streams are new in `example-global` (`checkout-load-test`, `stock-alerts`, `gift-card-jobs`), so its pages count 19 streams. `example-prod` collects subscription lists too, as the default system. `nats-eu-3` reports 14,212 subscriptions on its server page, the list cut at 10,000. A stream's subjects in `GetStream`'s configuration now follow the same rule as the Subjects page (none for a mirror, the bucket subjects for KV and object stores).
- **The JetStream API rate is not a sum (fix round 1).** Every server reports an account's API counters for the whole cluster, so summing over servers multiplied the rate by their number. The rate is the highest one server's increase over the 15 minutes, per server in its newest run, a run ending where the counter fell (another server's restart takes its share away), with that server's error share. `TestJetStreamAPITotalIsClusterWide` shows the counters agree across a 3-node cluster once the account stores data on any server of the cluster; until then each server reports only the requests it answered, mostly the meta leader, and the highest is a lower bound. A server that receives an update creates its own usage entry and sends updates too, with its own API counts and no stored data.
- **A restarted server's first reports are left out (fix round 2).** A new process reports 0 until the others' usage updates bring in the running total about 1.5 seconds later, so a collection in that second read the whole history as one interval's requests (3,372 a second for 3 in the reviewer's case). A server's reports within 60 seconds of its process start (`server_stats.start_time`) are left out; without a known start time a report is kept. A median of the servers was considered and is worse: before the counters are shared ([0 50 0]) it reads 0. On a tie the first server by ID wins, so the total and the errors come from one server. Known limit: when an account first stores data, every server takes in the others' totals at once, so the rate reads high for up to 15 minutes after the account's first stored message.
- **Each series' first report is left out too (fix round 3).** The 60-second rule missed a server whose clock is behind the collector's, a report without a start time, and a 0 held longer (routes up late, or the account's JetStream enabled late on that server). The first report ever of each server ID and account is now left out as well, and a missing `server_stats.start_time` falls back to `servers.start_time`.
- **The first-report look-back is bounded (fix round 4).** Looking up each series' first report in the whole history took 630 to 940 ms over 115 million reports (4 days, 50 servers, 200 accounts) and would take about 3 s over 14 days. A series now counts as new when it has no report in the hour before the window, and both scans are bounded by the epochs read first: 150 to 220 ms. A series that returns after a longer gap loses one report. Known limits: the first-stored-message jump above, routes that stay down across several collections, and a server clock skewed so that a report still holding its own count passes the 60-second rule after its first report. The settle rule and the start-time fallback have tests of their own (`TestSubjectsAPIRateSettle`, `TestSubjectsAPIRateStartFallback`), and the tie test now ties.
- **The demo's order streams' details follow the story (fix round 4).** `order-events` was created 77 days ago, so it has reached its 30-day limit, with sequences ending at 77 days of messages and starting 30 days of messages before; `order-archive`, made 48 days ago, keeps its origin's sequence numbers and ends at the same last sequence. Their consumers' delivered and ack floor sequences follow the stream's last sequence (fix round 5).
- **`SubjectMatch` and `CoreMatch` lose their account fields (fix round 2)**, which `MatchAccount` carries, and `SubjectMatch` gains `kind` (`SubjectRowKind`), so the tester can tell a KV bucket or object store from a stream. Fields are numbered in order.
- **The demo's mirror stores what its origin stores (fix rounds 2 and 3).** `order-events` and `order-archive` now tell one story: messages of 1,200 bytes stored at 11.39 a second, 1.1 GiB a day. `order-events`, at its 30-day age limit, holds 29,527,900 of them (33.0 GiB) and does not grow; `order-archive`, made 48 days ago, holds 47,244,640 (52.8 GiB) and grows 1.1 GiB a day, as Capacity and `nats-ap-2`'s store growth already said; its stored rate is its origin's. Before: `order-events` 9,204,411 messages, 11.2 GiB, +310 MiB a day, 15.2 a second set by hand; `order-archive` 61,200,400 messages, 52.4 GiB over 300 days, +1.1 GiB a day.
- **The tester answers per account (fix round 1).** A message reaches only the account it is published in. With an account, the answer is that account's, also when nothing matches. Without one, `MatchSubjectResponse.accounts` holds one `MatchAccount` per account where something matches, as if the message were published there, by account with the system account last; the system account answers only a subject that starts with `$SYS.`. `MatchAccount.jetstream_api` says the account's JetStream API answers a `$JS.API.` subject, in place of "nothing". The flat `matches` and `subscribers` of the response moved into the groups, and `SubjectMatch`'s fields are numbered in order. The demo has one account with streams and subscriptions besides `SYS`, so "two accounts give two groups" is a store test (`TestMatchSubjectFromStore`, with a `$G` stream) and the table row checks the grouping in every system.
- **The collection read is one with an answer.** The newest epoch in which a server answered SUBSZ, so an ask that went unanswered everywhere does not hide the collection before it.
- **Subjects with a control character** (NUL, vertical tab and the other C0 codes, DEL) are refused, as white space is.
- **Sources link by name only.** `stream_config.sources` keeps a source's name without its account or filter: a source from another account links to a same-named stream of the stream's own account, and a source filter is not applied. Left as it is.
- **The demo's `cluster.connections`** ends at 1,144 with the two `SYS` connections, as the answering servers and the Connections page with tooling count.
- **The merge.** `feat/subjects-collect` was merged into `feat/subjects-api` while T1 was in a fix round, once its migration 0032 existed; T1's later fixes change comments and validation, not tables.

### Where T3 departed from this plan

Each point was decided in the session that built T3 and is proposed, not yet confirmed.

- **Patterns are not links.** The Stored in cell links the stream; a pattern linking to the same page would give a row two links to one place. The stream names in a note (overlaps, mirror of, sourced into, mirrored by) link to their streams.
- **An account links to this page scoped to it.** No account page exists; the Account cell opens Subjects with `?account=` set, keeping the page's filter, and is plain text once the page is scoped.
- **Consumers link to the Consumers list.** "4 durable consumers" opens Consumers filtered to the stream, with ephemeral consumers shown when the stream has any; a connection name opens Connections filtered by that name, which includes tooling connections. That filter is "name contains", so `order-service` also lists `order-service-v2`; kept, as no exact-name filter exists.
- **The line under the title** reads "Where messages can go: stream subjects and client subscriptions, by subject pattern.", without the board's "service endpoints", which nothing collects yet (see Leftovers), and names the collection's time as Capacity does.
- **A note for mirrored streams.** T2's `mirrored_by` gives a note "mirrored by `<stream>`", after "sourced into", for a stream with no other note.
- **The tester card follows the server's groups.** One part per account the message is published in, in the server's order. Without a scope each part has a heading "Published in APP" (the system account tagged "system"); with a scope the heading is left out. A part lists its streams, their matching consumers, mirrors and sourcing streams, "the account's JetStream API answers it" when `jetstream_api` is set, and its core subscribers ("none", or "not collected" without a subscription collection). Who reads a stream follows its kind: "then consumers" with the durable names and "N ephemeral"; on a KV bucket "then read by" with "N watchers". Nobody reading reads "no consumer reads it" in the warning colours only on a stream that no mirror or sourcing stream copies (the table's no-consumer rule); a copied stream and an object store read "no consumer", a KV bucket "no watcher", without the warning. A queue group reads "one of 3 in queue group `<q>`", other subscriptions "136 connections", each with its names and "via `<subject>`". Queue group and connection names follow the `.sj-name` rule of the table (below). The card's body is one `aria-live="polite"` region for the card's life, so each answer is announced. With no collection in the range the card is not shown and the page's "No collection in this range." says it once; the card keeps the sentence for an answer that comes before the page's. A scoped account's empty part, and an empty answer without a scope, read "no stream stores it and no subscriber listens", or say subscribers are not known when subscriptions were not collected.
- **The tester's timing.** The field asks 300 ms after the last keystroke (the first character at once, by `useDebounced`); while the answer for the subject in the field is not in, the card says "Looking it up…" and shows no earlier answer. A refused subject shows the server's reason under the field, without the `invalid argument:` prefix, in a polite live region the field names with `aria-describedby`, and no card. The typed subject is not kept in the address.
- **The footnote's text.** The board's "every epoch" reads "every collection", the word the UI uses. Each further fact is a line of its own: the collection's time, servers that did not answer, lists cut at the limit, the servers' own subscriptions left out, and subscriptions of unlisted connections left out. Without a subscription collection only the notice above the table says "Subscriptions not collected", and the footnote keeps its sentence on rates but not the one on how subscription lists are refreshed.
- **The table's cells.** A row that is not a stream's (subscription, inbox, JetStream API) has a dash heard as "no stored rate" in Stored msgs/s; a stream without a known rate keeps "not observed". A pattern is plain text that can break at any character (`overflow-wrap:anywhere`), so a long token never overflows its column and a screen reader reads it as one subject; breaking only between tokens was tried in round 1 and dropped in round 2, as a long token overflowed and Chrome read the tokens with spaces between them. The column has room for short patterns: at 760 px or less the sticky column is a fixed 176 px (about 18 to 20 characters of the 13 px mono; the demo's patterns up to 20 characters stay whole, the 21- and 24-character ones wrap, at a hyphen where there is one); above 760 px it is at least 240 px (about 28 characters), and a longer pattern breaks inside the column instead of widening the table, as the round 2 `nowrap` rule did at 761 px, where the card is about 470 px wide. The trade-off: a pattern longer than the column breaks mid-word on a phone (decided in the session, proposed, not yet confirmed). A connection or queue group name is an inline box (`.sj-name`) that keeps the name, with its brackets, comma and "+N" (formatted as `1,201`), on one line unless it is wider than its box, in the table and the card; a plain `white-space:nowrap` would overflow a chip at phone width.
- **Pills without a collection** show no counts.
- **Pills.** The app's filter chips (as on Placement) rather than the board's round pills; the filter is `?filter=stored|no-consumer|overlapping|wide`.
