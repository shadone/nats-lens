# Capacity build plan

Working plan for the Capacity page (`docs/features/capacity.md`, board `Capacity.dc.html` of the design canvas). Everything here was proposed by the planning session of 2026-10-21 and is not yet confirmed by the owner. When a task lands, its lasting content moves into `docs/features/capacity.md`, `docs/features/triage-and-mutes.md` and `docs/design.md`; this file then only tracks status.

The board's sample names come from a sketch of a real deployment. Use the example shop of `inventory.example.yaml` and the demo's systems (`example-global`, `example-prod`, `example-staging`) instead; no name from the board goes into the repository.

## Rules for every step

- No collector change. `server_stats` (`js_storage`, `js_max_storage`, `js_enabled`), `stream_stats` (`bytes`, `messages`, `first_ts`, `num_subjects`, `is_leader`), `stream_config` (`retention`, `storage`, `replicas`, `max_msgs`, `max_bytes`, `max_age_ns`, `max_msgs_per_subject`, `discard`, `mirror`, `sources`, `allow_msg_ttl`), `findings`, `mutes` and `inventory` hold every value below. One migration, `0031_accepted_findings.sql` (T1), adds the acceptance columns to `mutes`.
- The page shows the newest collection at or before the end of the page range (the epoch rule of `GetPlacement`). Growth covers the whole page range, as on the Streams page.
- A value a server did not report is unset, never 0. An empty stream (no messages) has no `first_ts`: the writer already stores the zero time as NULL (`internal/store/values.go`), and the page reads it as "empty", not as a date.
- One growth rule on the page: `api.RobustPerDay` (Theil-Sen over the range, unset under 6 hours). See "Growth and time to full" below for why it is not the checks' rule.
- Acceptance is a kind of mute. Every place that reads mutes today (the Overview tally and status, the Fleet badges, `/metrics`, the status timeline's SQL, the archive's `mute_changes`) then treats an accepted finding as muted without a change of its own; the UI words it as accepted.

### Growth and time to full

The feature doc says forecasts use the checks' rule (the smaller of the two halves' slopes over a 6-hour lookback, both positive). The page uses `RobustPerDay` over the page range instead. Proposed, not yet confirmed, because:

- The card shows a rate and a date from it. With the checks' rule over 6 hours, a store that grows by day and stands still by night has no rate half the time, and "full in 3 years" turns into nothing an hour later; a page range of a day or a week smooths that.
- The Streams page and the storage tile already show `RobustPerDay`; the same stream would otherwise show two different growth numbers on two pages.
- Theil-Sen is the median of pairwise slopes, so a one-off step (a restore, a purge) moves it no more than the halves rule is meant to stop.
- The checks keep their rule for firing. When `server-014` or `stream-006` fires, the card or row shows the check's own days beside the link, named as the check's, so a person sees both numbers and where each comes from.

### Next limit

Per row, in this order:

1. No messages: "empty".
2. A limit reached now: the oldest message at 95% of `max_age` or more, or `messages` at 99% of `max_msgs` or more, or `bytes` at 99% of `max_bytes` or more. With discard old (or a limits stream): "steady at 30 days" (or "at 1M msgs"); with discard new: "1M msgs: reached, refuses new messages", in the critical colour.
3. Else, for each limit that is set, the days until it is reached: age `max_age − held`; messages and bytes from the leader's growth (`RobustPerDay` of `messages` and of `bytes` over the range), when above 0. The soonest: "7 days: in 3 days", "8 GiB: in 41 days" ("then refuses new messages" with discard new). A limit without a forecast reads "1M msgs: not reached" (the first in the order age, messages, bytes).
4. Only a per-subject limit: "per subject only: not forecast" (nats-lens does not see per-subject counts).
5. No limit: "none: grows until the store is full".

The thresholds of 95% and 99% are proposed, not yet confirmed: age is measured at collections up to one interval apart, and a counter limit is enforced exactly.

## T1 Accept a finding as by design

The first part of "Accept a finding with a review date" of `docs/features/checks.md` and `docs/features/triage-and-mutes.md`, scoped to what the board needs.

- **Scope.** One finding of one entity: a reason (at most 1000 characters), who accepted it (IAP identity or the typed name, as a mute), and a review date between one day and 365 days ahead. The acceptance applies when findings are read, as a mute does, and ends early when the entity's growth changes by more than 50% (below). The API accepts any check for one entity; the Capacity page offers it only for `stream-005`. Not in this task: acceptance of related entities or of a check everywhere, a "Review accepted findings" list, accepting from the finding page; each goes to the leftovers.
- **Why `stream-005` only on the page.** `stream-005` is the finding of an unbounded stream, which is what "by design" means on the board. `stream-006` is a forecast that the stream will refuse or drop messages soon, and `server-014` that a whole store fills; accepting either hides an outage on its way, and a mute with an end in at most 90 days is the right tool for those. Proposed, not yet confirmed.
- **Storage.** Migration `0031_accepted_findings.sql`: `mutes.accepted BOOLEAN DEFAULT false` (NULL read as false, for rows from before), `mutes.growth_per_day DOUBLE` (the entity's growth at acceptance, bytes per day; NULL when it had no rate), `mutes.end_reason VARCHAR` (why nats-lens ended it; NULL when a person lifted it or it ran out), with comments, and a new `COMMENT ON TABLE mutes` saying that an accepted row is an acceptance. An accepted row has an entity; the API refuses an acceptance without one, in Go, rather than by a table check. `mutes` is in the archive: the implementer checks that a rebuild from Parquet written before 0031 still loads (`internal/store/archive.go`), and says how.
- **API.** `MuteFindingRequest` gains `bool accept` (next free number, 7) and `Mute` gains `bool accepted`, `optional double growth_per_day` and `string end_reason` (10, 11, 12). With `accept`: the entity is required, `expires_at` (the review date) is at most 365 days ahead (`MaxAcceptLength` beside `MaxMuteLength` in `internal/api/normalize.go`), and the server, not the client, records `growth_per_day`: for a stream the leader's bytes, for a server `js_storage`; other entities record none. (Superseded: the rule is the week's growth, see "Where T1 departed from this plan".) Lifting stays `UnmuteFinding`; `ListMutes` lists acceptances with the mutes. `Finding.mute` carries the acceptance, so every reader of mutes sees it.
- **Growth review.** After each check run (`Runner.check` in `internal/runner/runner.go`), every acceptance in force is compared with the entity's growth by the same rule (superseded: the week's growth, and an acceptance without a recorded growth gets a baseline; see "Where T1 departed from this plan"). When both are known and they differ by more than half of the larger of the recorded rate's size and 1 MiB per day, nats-lens ends it: `unmuted_at` the epoch's time, `unmuted_by` "nats-lens", `end_reason` "growth changed from +2.1 MiB/day to +4.0 MiB/day". The 1 MiB floor keeps a bucket accepted at "+0" from returning on a few KiB; it is proposed, not yet confirmed. A growth that cannot be measured now (under 6 hours of data, the stream gone) ends nothing. At the review date the acceptance runs out as a mute does, and the finding counts again if it still fires.
- **Demo.** `fake` MuteFinding and UnmuteFinding keep `accept`, `growth_per_day` (from the stream's `perDay` in the scenario) and `end_reason`; the growth review is not simulated, but the seeded mutes gain one acceptance in force and one ended by nats-lens with its `end_reason` (T2 picks the streams). `TestDemoUsesRealChecks` covers the seeded acceptances.
- **Tests.** Normalize: accept without an entity, a review date of 366 days, 365 days accepted; growth recorded by the server and a client value ignored; the runner ends an acceptance on a change of 60%, keeps it on 40%, keeps it under the 1 MiB floor, keeps it when growth is unset; an accepted finding reads as muted in `Findings`, the tally, the timeline and `/metrics`; the migration applies on a store with mutes, and an archive rebuild from old Parquet loads. A conformance row: a finding under an acceptance, in both providers.
- **Docs.** `docs/design.md` (acceptance as a kind of mute, the growth review and its floor), `triage-and-mutes.md` and `checks.md` stay planned for the parts not built; T4 moves what shipped. `checks/stream-005.sql`'s remediation adds "or accept it as by design on the Capacity page" to the inventory and mute advice.

### Where T1 departed from this plan

Each point was decided by the session that built T1: proposed, not yet confirmed.

- **Migration column order.** `accepted BOOLEAN DEFAULT false` is added last in `0031_accepted_findings.sql`: DuckDB refuses to commit a transaction in which another `ALTER` follows `ADD COLUMN ... DEFAULT` on a table that holds rows (seen on a store with mutes; `TestMigrationsFrom30OnMutes`). Rows from before the migration and rows loaded from older Parquet files get `false` from the default, not NULL; readers still read NULL as false.
- **System entity refused.** An acceptance needs an entity with a key, so the system entity is refused as invalid. A plain mute of the system entity fails today as an internal error (its NULL key breaks the table's check that type and key are both set or both NULL); that is older than T1 and is left for its own fix.
- **No client growth to ignore.** `MuteFindingRequest` has no growth field, so a client cannot send one; the tests show the recorded value is the rate of the stored data instead.
- **Growth rule (fix round 2).** Not `RobustPerDay` over 24 hours, and not the day-over-day median of round 1. Theil-Sen over one 24-hour window sees a daily rhythm as a changing rate (326 of 576 pairs of acceptance and review hours ended a business-hours stream); the day-over-day median over 7 days still ended acceptances on a weekly sawtooth (1632 of 3136 pairs), on weekday-only growth, and 2 to 5 days after a purge, and recorded -190 MiB/day from 25 hours of data with a purge. Both sides now use `api.WeeklyGrowth`: the median, over the bucket points of the last 24 hours, of (v(p) − v(p − 7 days)) / 7, with v(p − 7 days) interpolated, never across a gap of more than 3× the bucket spacing. Daily and weekly patterns cancel exactly; the review reads two spans of a day (the last day and the day a week before), not 8 days.
- **Young entities and outages.** The paired points must cover two thirds of the last day, a stretch between two of them counting only up to 3 bucket widths (fix round 3: after a 22 h outage, 1 to 3 pairs decided and a 5 h cycle of ±60 MiB ended 72 of 120 restarts; fix round 4: coverage replaced a count of day / width buckets, which failed at intervals of 48 minutes or more when epochs sit within jitter of the bucket grid, two in one bucket and none in the next). So it needs about 8 days of history and a mostly collected last day. Without it, `growth_per_day` stays NULL at acceptance and the review writes the first measurable rate as the baseline, ending nothing that time, by a conditional update that leaves a lifted acceptance alone.
- **Purges end acceptances.** A purge or a restore changes the week's net growth and ends the acceptance within a day. That is intended (proposed, not yet confirmed): the finding returns and a person can accept it again.
- **End reason.** "growth changed from +10 MiB/day to +16 MiB/day over the last 7 days (+92 MiB in the last day)": the week's figure is the rate compared, which at the moment of ending is still between the old and the new rate; the last day's change shows the new rate of a recent change. The coordinator's proposed wording "(over the last 7 days)" alone would have read "to +16" for a change to +100.
- **Capacity page.** The acceptance's rate can differ from the page's "/day" (`RobustPerDay` over the page range); the end reason names its measure.
- **Archive (fix round 3).** A baseline the review writes counts in `mute_changes` (acceptances with a growth are added to the count), so the newest hour is exported again with it, and a rebuild keeps, among copies not lifted, the one with the growth (`unmuted_at DESC NULLS LAST, growth_per_day IS NULL`); before, the copies tied and a rebuild lost the baseline in about half the runs, which delayed an end until the review baselined again. Files from before 0031 get the column as NULL for that order. Proposed, not yet confirmed.
- **Review timing.** The review runs only after a check run that succeeded, and skips an acceptance created after the epoch started, so it never ends one at a time before its creation.
- **`stream-005` remediation.** It says "or accept it as by design with a review date" without "on the Capacity page", as the page arrives with T3; T3 adds the page's name.
- **Demo.** The fake records the growth of an accepted stream with `RobustPerDay` over 8 days of its scenario series (`scen.growth`), not `WeeklyGrowth`; the demo's acceptances are being reworked with T2. Seeded: in `example-prod` a new `stream-005` finding on `KV_customer-config`, accepted in force by `ops@example.com` with a review date six months later; in `example-global` an acceptance of `stream-005` on `KV_feature-flags` ended by nats-lens (its end reason built by `api.GrowthEndReason`). The fake timeline has no row for the new finding: it does not apply mutes to its rows at all, so T2 adds the row if the page needs it. `fake_test.go`'s counts of the prod scenario (muted 2, 7 findings with muted, 3 mutes) changed with it.

### Where T2 departed from this plan

Each point was decided by the session that built T2: proposed, not yet confirmed.

- **order-jobs' next limit.** By the next-limit rule an age limit always has a forecast while the stream holds messages, so `example-prod`'s `order-jobs` (7 days, 1M msgs, oldest message 4 days 6 hours old) reads "7 days: in 2.75 days", not "1M msgs: not reached". "Not reached" is shown by `example-staging`'s `order-jobs` (1M msgs, no growth) and the store fixture's shrinking `KV_shrink`.
- **audit-archive's days.** The demo keeps the `stream-006` it had (13.1 days); the page's own forecast from its growth agrees. Its `stream-006` is muted (the prod seed mute), and a muted `stream-006` still reads as fills soon, with its days and the mute in `mute`: the Status table has no row for it. It sorts after the unmuted fills-soon rows and after those awaiting a decision (fix round 1 of the review), so with more than 500 rows awaiting a decision a muted fills-soon row can fall past the cut.
- **Reached limits.** When several limits are reached at once, one that refuses messages (discard new) comes before one that drops the oldest; otherwise the order is age, messages, bytes. Age reached always reads steady, whatever the discard policy.
- **Field shapes.** A limit is set only when above 0 (unset: no limit), not -1 as the server reports. `NextLimit.state` is unset for none and empty. `declared_reason` is a code, `inventory` or `writer_ttls`, for the UI to word. `CapacityStatus` has the states of the Status table, named `FILLS_SOON`, `ACCEPTED`, `DECLARED`, `MUTED`, `AWAITING_DECISION`, `UNBOUNDED`, `BOUNDED`.
- **Server cards.** A card for every server the epoch named (expected, observed or missed) or that reported in it; one without JetStream numbers in the epoch is not reported, and has no `per_day` or `days_to_full` either (fix round 1). Servers that answered with JetStream disabled, or that the `servers` table records without JetStream, are counted in `servers_without_jetstream`. `js_max_storage` of 0 leaves `limit` unset.
- **Numbers and growth.** A row's numbers are `ListStreams`': the leader's report, else the largest any server reports, not the first reporting server by name, so a leaderless stream shows the same bytes on both pages (fix round 1). The first message and subjects are the leader's whenever it reports, else the earliest and the most (fix round 2). Its growth is the Streams page's (`coalesce(leader, largest)` per epoch): `streamPoints` reads bytes and messages for the Capacity page, and `ListStreams` keeps its bytes-only `streamGrowth`; both are built from shared SQL pieces and a test pins them to one number (fix round 2); a conformance row checks that bytes, messages and growth agree in every system.
- **A cut list in conformance.** Neither the demo nor the store fixture holds more than 500 streams, so the conformance row holds in every system: at most 500 rows, all of the total when under 500, the streams of `ListStreams` with its growth. The cut itself is tested on `FinishCapacity`, which both providers share. Kind rows add "sources" beside the plan's six.
- **The demo story.** Fixes from T1's review: `KV_customer-config` has no `max_bytes` in its bucket spec (its accepted `stream-005` was contradicted by a 64 MiB limit), and `KV_feature-flags` was accepted at +2.0 KiB/day; a release made it grow +1.6 MiB/day from 2026-10-11, and the review ended the acceptance at the first collection its week's growth (`WeeklyGrowth`, after the merge of T1) changed by more than the floor's half, 2026-10-13 17:00: "growth changed from +2.0 KiB/day to +520 KiB/day over the last 7 days (+1.6 MiB in the last day)", written by the review's own rule over the bucket's series. Its `stream-005` fires again. Every demo `stream-005` is written from the specs as the check writes it, and a test checks findings, mutes and acceptances against the specs and series. Besides the plan: `example-global`'s `order-archive` is a mirror of `order-events` and gains a `stream-005` and its mute; `KV_session-cache` loses its 512 MiB `max_bytes`, so it is declared by its writers' TTLs; `price-changes` stands at its message limit with discard new (refusing) and no longer grows; `search-index` sources two streams; `example-prod`'s stores grow 17 MiB a day each (65 before; the storage tile's series follows), `order-callbacks` is empty, `KV_order-jobs-cache` gains a 48-hour TTL, and `example-staging`'s `KV_customer-config` is listed with 64 MiB. The fake timeline gains `stream-005` rows in `example-prod` and `example-global`, one row per check over both entities.
