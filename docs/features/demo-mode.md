# Demo mode

- **Surfaces:** `ui`, `api`, `cli`
- **Status:** shipped
- **Related:** [data-history.md](data-history.md), [overview.md](overview.md), [running-nats-lens.md](running-nats-lens.md), [overview-page.md](overview-page.md), [multiple-systems.md](multiple-systems.md), [design.md](../design.md)

## What it does

`natslens demo` serves the web UI and the API with three fixed systems, without NATS and without a database. `example-prod`, the default, is a three-server cluster a few hours after a Kubernetes node upgrade restarted all three servers. `example-global` is a supercluster of 24 servers in the clusters `eu`, `us` and `ap`, joined by gateways, after a node upgrade left one server down. `example-staging` has been watched for nine hours and has stopped collecting: its credentials were rejected three hours ago. The demo is for seeing what nats-lens shows before connecting it to a real system, and for working on the UI without a cluster.

## Behavior and rules

- **Data history examples.** `example-staging` has nine hours of history, a three-minute collector restart about five hours before now, and a collector connection closed three hours before now. Collections and series omit the restart interval. It has 14-day database retention and no archive. `example-prod` and `example-global` have 30-day database retention; their archive examples keep hours for 90 days, holding 90 and 30 days respectively. `example-global` also has a five-minute collection gap and a four-minute Kubernetes-watch gap. Data history lists these gaps and history requirements, and charts hatch collection losses; Fleet keeps its separate status strips.

- **No notification channels.** `natslens demo` reads no configuration file and sends no notifications. The notification delivery check has no queued messages and does not fire.
- **No NATS, no database.** The demo answers every API call from built-in scenarios; nothing is collected or stored on disk.
- **Three systems.** `ListSystems` lists `example-prod`, `example-global` and `example-staging` in that order, and a call without a `system` reads `example-prod`. Each system has its own servers, streams, consumers, findings, events, timeline and series, and its own mutes.
- **example-prod.** Three servers in one cluster with 187 client connections, 100 of them on `nats-0`; the node upgrade restarted each server, an expected durable consumer was deleted (`consumer-010`), one consumer's copies on the servers were created at different times (`consumer-009`), and a backlog grows (`consumer-002`, flapping).
- **example-global.** 24 servers: 9 in `eu`, 9 in `us` and 6 in `ap`, with the meta leader in `eu`. A node upgrade of the `us` pool restarted three servers and left `nats-us-4` down, so two streams run on two of their three replicas. `nats-ap-2`'s store is 71% full and full in under 14 days at its growth (`server-014`), two `ap` servers run an older version (a muted finding), `nats-ap-3` runs with a larger maximum payload than the others, and a client is dropped as a slow consumer on `nats-us-3`. `nats-us-4`'s pod is Pending on no node. `nats-ap-2` also runs at about 70% CPU, so it is the busiest server of the CPU band.
- **Every placement square.** `example-global` also shows the replica states that only arise in trouble: `nats-ap-4` kept an old `max_age` of `customer-notifications` (a disagreeing square, "servers disagree" and `stream-007`), `nats-us-3` came back from the upgrade without its replica of `return-requests` (a missing square and `stream-010`), and `basket-events` has no leader in the newest collection (squares of unknown state, no finding until a second collection without one).
- **A story to replay.** `example-global`'s `us` pool upgrade, 08:44 to 09:20 in the scenario's frame, holds what the Replay page tells: `nats-us-1` to `nats-us-3` announce lame duck mode a second after their pods are killed, the stream and consumer leaders they held are elected on other servers, their clients disconnect and connect to the new process, and each comes back with the streams and consumers it held before, or fewer. `nats-us-4`'s leaders are elected elsewhere when it shuts down. The durables `restock-alerts` and `stock-audit-export` on `inventory-changes` are held by `nats-us-0` to `nats-us-2` until `nats-us-2`'s restart and by no server after, without a delete event; `consumer-013` fires for each a minute later and resolves an hour after their last report, after the window. `nats-us-4` stops answering and takes `KV_label-templates`, its only replica, with it. `example-prod`'s deleted `analytics-aggregator` is a gone asset with a delete event that names the client, and a moment after `example-staging`'s data ended is a time without collections.
- **Links to draw.** The Topology page and `GetTopology` answer for every demo system. `example-global`: a gateway from every server to each other cluster, `eu`-`us` 84 ms, `eu`-`ap` 166 ms and `ap`-`us` 212 ms, with `nats-ap-1`'s gateway to `us` reconnecting three times in the last hour (13:41, 13:58, 14:17); the gateways to `nats-us-1` to `nats-us-4` moved to other servers when the upgrade stopped them, drops of their reporters on the day. Three pooled routes between any two servers of a cluster, at 0.3 to 0.6 ms in `eu` and `us` and about 4.6 ms in `ap`, whose servers lie further apart; `nats-eu-3`'s at 4 ms stand out as slow, and `ap`'s, all alike, do not. 30 warehouses and pickup points of the shop as leaf node sites in account `APP` on `eu` and `us`: `pickup-0417` dropped 17 times in the last day and has not connected since 13:52, `warehouse-eu-03` reconnected twice, `pickup-0420` connected in the newest collection, so its drops are not counted yet, and the sites on the `us` servers the upgrade stopped moved to other hubs without a drop. `example-prod` and `example-staging` are one cluster each with routes only, `example-prod` with a route of its own for `APP` besides the pool; `example-staging` reads its last collection, three hours old. The Server page lists the same routes and round trips and counts each server's leaf node sites, at the range's collection as the Topology page reads it.
- **Accounts and logins.** `GetAccountsAndAuth` answers for every demo system, and the Accounts page shows it ([accounts-and-auth.md](accounts-and-auth.md)). `example-global` runs in operator mode with three accounts: `APP` with a limit of 2,000 connections and 500 GiB of JetStream disk, 16 GiB of memory and a JWT that never expires, the system account `SYS`, and `SUPPLIERS`, idle, with an export of `inventory.changes` (which `APP` imports) and a JWT that expires in 12 days; JetStream is disabled for `SYS` and `SUPPLIERS` (disk and memory limits of 0). `SUPPLIERS` is named by an account public key; `APP` and `SYS` keep their names as identifiers, as on every other page. Its failed logins: `scale-terminal-07`, a warehouse scale at `10.20.4.31` whose credentials were revoked yesterday at 12:00, every minute, on `nats-us-3` or, while that restarted, `nats-us-0` ("User Authentication Revoked", 1,440 in the last 24 hours), and a client without a user from `203.0.113.12`, 8 times every 2 hours ("Authentication Timeout", 96). They are authentication error events on the Events page too. `nats-eu-0`'s client listener certificate expires in 16 days, and the `pos-terminal` user JWT of three `checkout-service` connections in 5 days; the other certificates expire in 75 days, `nats-us-4`'s read from its last collection before it stopped. The two JWTs fire their checks as in a store: `account-003` for `SUPPLIERS` and `account-004` for `APP`, warnings since each came within 14 days of its expiry and fire longer than `nats-us-4`'s outage, which still heads the system as an expiry warning ranks after the other warnings; the certificate, 16 days out, fires no `server-016`. `example-prod` runs in config mode: `APP` alone, looked up, without a JWT, and certificates that expire in 58 days. `example-staging`'s `APP` was never looked up.
- **Capacity.** `GetCapacity` answers for every demo system. `example-prod`: three stores a few percent full that fill in more than 5 years at their growth; a stream of every kind (`order-jobs` reaching its 7 days, `catalog-changes` steady at 30 days, the work queue `order-callbacks` empty, `media-windows` an interest stream, the memory bucket `KV_worker-presence`, the object stores at their 48-hour TTL); `order-archive` without a limit, awaiting a decision; `KV_customer-config`, a bucket without a TTL or byte limit, accepted as by design by `ops@example.com` with a review in six months; `audit-archive` filling its byte limit, which refuses new messages, with its muted `stream-006`. `example-global`: `nats-ap-2` filling by `server-014`, `nats-us-4` not reported; `order-archive`, a mirror of `order-events` without a limit, muted while its retention is decided; `KV_feature-flags`, accepted at +2.0 KiB/day and ended by nats-lens two and a half days after a release made it grow +1.6 MiB/day, when its week's growth reached +516 KiB/day, awaiting a decision again; `KV_session-cache`, bounded by its writers' TTLs, shrinking; `price-changes` at its message limit, refusing new messages. `example-staging`: no growth anywhere (under 6 hours of data), `order-jobs` not reaching its message limit and `catalog-changes` awaiting a decision. A stream without a limit and without a finding is shown only by the test database.
- **Subjects.** `GetSubjects` and `MatchSubject` answer for every demo system, and the Subjects page shows them ([subjects.md](subjects.md)). Every stream lists its subjects: its name with dots for dashes and `.>` (`order-events` stores `order.events.>`), a KV bucket's `$KV.<bucket>.>`, an object store's `$O.<store>.C.>` and `$O.<store>.M.>`, none for a mirror. `example-global` collected its subscription lists 5 minutes apart: `checkout-load-test`, written by last week's load test, has no consumer (and `basket-events` none either); `stock-alerts` keeps low-stock alerts only while `inventory-service` listens through an ephemeral push consumer, whose connection the row names; the work queue `gift-card-jobs` is empty; `KV_feature-flags`'s 22 watchers name their connections; `order-archive` mirrors `order-events`; three `order-service` connections share the queue group `order-service` on `orders.place`, and the services' request subjects (`checkout.quote`, `shipping.rates.request`, `search.query`) have queue groups of their own; the reply inboxes of several clients fold into one row; `notification-worker` listens on `customer.notifications.>`. In the system account `SYS`, `natslens-example-global` on `nats-us-0` subscribes to `$SYS.ACCOUNT.>` and `$SYS.SERVER.>`, and `ops-monitor` on `nats-eu-0` to `>`: the only connections in `SYS`, the first hidden as tooling on the Connections page. `nats-eu-3`'s list was cut at 10,000 of 14,212, `nats-us-4` did not answer, and the servers' own subscriptions make up the rest of `SYS`'s 412. `APP`'s JetStream API takes 4.6 requests a second, 0.13% failing. The tester answers for `APP` alone without a scope, and for `SYS`'s two connections only on a `$SYS.` subject. No two streams overlap, as NATS refuses that within an account. `example-prod` collects subscription lists too, with its clients' inboxes and queue groups and `analytics-worker` on `order.archive.>`; `example-staging` collected none, and as its data ended three hours ago it has no stored or API rate.
- **Micro services.** `GetMicroServices` answers for every demo system, and the Micro services page shows it ([micro-services.md](micro-services.md)). `example-global` asks `APP`, where three services built on the micro framework run on existing client connections of `eu` servers: `orders` (1.4.2, three instances on `order-service` connections), whose `get` endpoint on `orders.get.*` has taken ten times its 7-day level of 4.1 ms since 08:12 and whose `create` endpoint last failed with "409 duplicate order id"; `pricing` (four instances on `checkout-service` connections, three on 2.0.0 and one on 1.9.3), one of which restarted in place at 14:21 without a gap in the rate; and `inventory` (0.9.1, two instances on `inventory-service`), healthy now, though one instance hung from 10:05 to 10:47 and was missing then, still subscribed. 161.7, 88.0 and 12.6 requests a second. `SUPPLIERS` is asked too and refuses (a permissions violation), so it reads as asked without an answer: every 10 minutes its requests are sent and refused, and in between they are held, naming the refusal, when it came and when they will be sent again (the newest collection shows a request refused 5 minutes before); the demo names it `SUPPLIERS` on every page, as the store names accounts. `example-prod` does not ask its account: `catalog` (on `catalog-api`) and `ledger` (on `ledger-service`) are known from their `$SRV` subscriptions only, and while the node upgrade stopped a server, the instance on it was missing. `example-staging` runs none. Every instance's nine `$SRV` subscriptions and its endpoints' subjects in queue group `q` are rows of the Subjects page. In a system that collects subscription lists, a connection's subscription count on the Connections page is what the Subjects page lists for it.
- **Applications.** `GetApplications` answers for every demo system, and the Applications page shows it ([applications.md](applications.md)). Its connections are the Connections page's, its 24-hour counts come from the Events page's events, and its client findings are the system's. `example-prod`: after the upgrade the clients that left `nats-1` and `nats-2` stayed on `nats-0`, which holds 100 of the 187 connections, so a restart of it would move most of them at once. `resize-worker` names each pod's connection `resize-worker@<pod>`: four pods on three servers, one of them connected at 11:22 after it waited for a node; a connection is listed, and counted by its server, only from when it connected. `callback-dispatcher` stopped answering pings six times in the day ("Stale Connection") and came back each time, the last three within 10 minutes, so `client-004` fires and it is listed first. A script connects without a name at 10:05, so the `(unnamed)` application has no traffic in the hours before. The go clients report go 1.54.0 and the others nats.js 3.0.2. While `nats-0` was down during the upgrade (09:16 to 09:18) the page is partial, names it, and its spread does not speak. `example-global`: `shipping-service` on the `ap` servers still runs nats.js 2.29.3, so its row and the libraries summary show two versions, and runs at the carrier's site 140 ms from the servers, so `client-001` fires on it, muted as expected; it is listed on the row, which it does not lift; `label-printer`'s slow consumer disconnects count in the last day; `natslens-example-global` is hidden as tooling and `ops-monitor` is the only application of `SYS`. `example-staging`'s system events connection lost its server for 50 seconds four hours before it stopped, so its page says the 24-hour counts may be low. A range five days back has no captured event, so its counts are unset. A connection's message and byte counters grow from its start at a rate of its own times a level that follows the day, highest at 14:00, and the hourly traffic sums the same rates over the time its server was up, so an hour is what the counters grew by. `example-prod`'s slow consumers are counted by its servers but have no disconnect events, so its Applications page counts none. The Overview's clients tile counts the applications of the newest collection's connections, tooling and unnamed ones left out, as for real data.
- **Assets and leaders over time.** The vanished durables and `example-prod`'s deleted consumer are listed on the Consumers and Placement pages and in the stream detail for a range whose newest collection is before they went, and not after; the durable consumer count counts them until then. Every page reads the leaders of the range's newest collection, and they change only at an election event: when a leader stops answering, its assets have no leader until one is elected, by the scenario's own events or, where it writes none within 90 seconds, by the demo, which elects the first replica that answers (a consumer its stream's new leader) and writes that election as an event. The leader's "since" is the election that made it leader, or its process's start. Every other asset is the same in every range.
- **Kubernetes from its events.** Each node is cordoned, drained as its NATS pod leaves, upgraded and uncordoned, each event with the node, its node pool and its kubelet version in the payload as the watcher writes them, so Replay counts nodes and node pools. The timeline's Kubernetes lane and the node upgrade windows come from these events by the store's rules (`example-prod` 09:16 to 09:44, `example-global` 08:44 to 09:20), and the NATS lane draws the kinds the store draws. A node pool's own report of its upgrade names no node and is kept as a Kubernetes event of no kind of its own.
- **Changed findings.** `nats-us-4` announced its shutdown, so its `server-001` and the findings for the assets on it (`stream-002`, `stream-011`) fired as info while that counted as a planned restart and changed to warning when the restart window ended; they show the changed state, both transitions and info on the timeline before the change.
- **Every delivery rate.** Besides consumers that deliver, `example-global` has `reorder-planner`, paused between its nightly runs, at 0 per second, and `invoice-renderer`, created in the newest collection, without a rate yet. The missing consumer of `example-prod` has none either.
- **Growth as the store computes it.** Growth per day is the median of the slopes between pairs of points of each stream's own bytes over the range, as for real data: it is not shown for a range under 6 hours, nor for `example-staging`, whose data in any range spans less than 6 hours. `KV_session-cache` in `example-global` shrinks, so its growth is negative.
- **example-staging.** Three servers whose connections were closed after their credentials were rejected three hours ago: the collector's and the two that capture events, so Replay says events were not captured since then. Its summary says "not collecting" with the error and when it began, and its data, timeline and series end three hours before now. Its history began nine hours before now, inside the default range: its series, strip, finding and events start then, and `GetLensInfo` gives that time as `history_start`. The other systems' history reaches 30 days back. It has no Kubernetes watcher, so its servers have no pod placement.
- **Shifted to now.** By default the scenario is moved in time so that its "now" is the current time, and the live ranges work as with real data. `-fixed` keeps the scenario at its own time, 2026-10-17 14:30 UTC, so every response is the same on every run. Its clock stands still there: `GetLensInfo` reports that time as `now`, the UI reads it before it shows the shell, and every live range ends there and starts a preset before it, so the default "Last 24h" is 16 Oct 14:30 to 17 Oct 14:30 UTC on every page and in every refresh. `GetLensInfo` says so with `now_fixed`, which only the fixed demo sets, and the UI's clock stops there (proposed, not yet confirmed).

- **The same shapes as real data.** The scenario uses the same event kinds, check codes and API messages as a real collector. ## Scenarios

### See nats-lens without a cluster

- **Surfaces:** `cli`, `ui`
- **Given** a built `natslens` binary
- **When** I run `natslens demo` and open http://127.0.0.1:8080
- **Then** the Overview shows the scenario's findings, tiles, timeline with the node upgrade window, events, streams and servers, with "demo" in the sidebar

### See several systems

- **Surfaces:** `api`
- **Given** the demo is running
- **When** I call `ListSystems`
- **Then** it lists `example-prod`, `example-global` with 24 servers in 3 clusters of which 23 answer, and `example-staging` as not collecting, with "nats: authorization violation" since three hours ago

### See the states trouble brings

- **Surfaces:** `ui`, `api`

### Replay the node pool upgrade

- **Surfaces:** `api`

## Not supported / out of scope

- **No other scenarios.** The demo has three built-in systems; it cannot load a recorded database, and its systems cannot be configured.
- **No collection.** The demo never connects to NATS.
- **No persistence.** Mutes and acknowledgements made in the demo are lost when it stops.
